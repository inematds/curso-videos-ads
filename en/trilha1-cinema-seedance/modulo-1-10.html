Seedance Cinema · Module 10/10

UGC Ads III · The Real Product, 30 Seconds

One screenshot in, one 30-second ad out.

One screenshot in, one 30-second ad out. The ready-to-use version: upload a single product screenshot as an image reference, set the generation to 30 seconds and vertical 9:16, and paste this entire prompt. The dialogue adapts to the product you referenced — the brackets are optional substitutions, not homework.

A 30-second vertical UGC product ad, 9:16, starring the exact product from the reference image, with its real packaging, colors, and label perfectly preserved and clearly legible in every single shot. The entire video feels like it was filmed on a phone: handheld, natural daylight, a lived-in home, the subtle room echo of a phone microphone. AUDIO: no music — just her natural voice, room tone, and real foley sounds. It should feel like a real customer's video, not a produced ad.

SCENE ONE, the hook (0–6s): a relatable woman in her early thirties, with a messy bun and a casual sweater, opens a delivery box on the kitchen counter and takes out the reference product; she reacts off-camera in a natural, lip-synced voice: "[Okay — it finally came.]" Genuine curiosity, no sales energy.

SCENE TWO, first impression (6–13s): selfie-style framing, she holds the product next to her face, looks at it, and tells the camera what it is in her own casual words, naming the product from the label, with an honest first reaction: "[So this is the — reads the label — and everyone kept talking about it.]"

SCENE THREE, the demonstration (13–21s): three quick, tactile close-ups of a hand using the reference product the way this type of product is used — opening it, a close-up of the texture or contents, and using it in action — each shot landing with real foley sounds, keeping the label visible and accurate.

SCENE FOUR, the verdict (21–27s): back to the selfie-style framing, a little closer, natural window light; she shares one specific, honest benefit in her own words, as if telling a friend, with a small laugh: "[I genuinely didn't think it would — one real benefit.]"

SCENE FIVE, the sign-off (27–30s): she holds the product with the label facing the lens, taps it, and says with a warm shrug: "[Just get it. Thank me later.]" Hold on the label for a beat.

Include natural imperfections throughout the video: a small framing adjustment, a blink and a smile, third-take energy. No on-screen text, no logos anywhere except the product's actual label.

Make it your own: a 30-second master video costs 195 credits, so draft a 10-second version first and check ONE thing: the label. If it distorts, replace it with a cleaner, flatter screenshot as a reference — re-reference, never re-describe. Leave all the brackets intact and the model will fill in the lines based on the product itself.
